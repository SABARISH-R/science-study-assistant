# Colligative Properties

## What are colligative properties
Colligative properties are properties of a solution that depend on the number of dissolved solute particles, not on what the particles are. The four main ones are vapour pressure lowering, boiling point elevation, freezing point depression and osmotic pressure.

## Boiling point elevation
Adding a non-volatile solute raises the boiling point of the solvent. The rise is proportional to the molality of the solute, using the relation delta T = i x Kb x m, where Kb is a constant for the solvent and i is the van 't Hoff factor.

## Freezing point depression
Dissolving a solute lowers the freezing point of the solvent. This is why salt is spread on icy roads: salty water freezes at a lower temperature than pure water. The depression is delta T = i x Kf x m.

## Osmosis and osmotic pressure
Osmosis is the movement of solvent through a semipermeable membrane from a dilute solution to a more concentrated one. Osmotic pressure is the pressure needed to stop this flow, and it increases with the concentration of solute particles.

## Van 't Hoff factor
The van 't Hoff factor i counts how many particles one formula unit produces in solution. Glucose does not dissociate, so i is about 1, while sodium chloride gives two ions, so i is close to 2. More particles mean a larger colligative effect.

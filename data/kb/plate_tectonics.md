# Plate Tectonics

## What are tectonic plates
Earth's outer shell, the lithosphere, is broken into large rigid pieces called tectonic plates. They float on the softer, slowly flowing asthenosphere beneath them and move a few centimetres each year, about as fast as fingernails grow.

## Why plates move
Heat from Earth's interior drives mantle convection, where hotter material rises and cooler material sinks. Plates are also pulled by their own weight at subduction zones (slab pull) and pushed away from mid-ocean ridges (ridge push).

## Types of plate boundaries
At divergent boundaries plates move apart and new crust forms. At convergent boundaries plates collide, and one may sink beneath the other in subduction or both may crumple into mountains. At transform boundaries plates slide past each other, as along the San Andreas Fault.

## Earthquakes and volcanoes
Most earthquakes and volcanoes occur along plate boundaries. Stress builds as plates stick, then releases suddenly as an earthquake. Volcanoes form where magma rises, often above subduction zones or at divergent boundaries.

# Electroplating

## What is electroplating
Electroplating is a process that coats an object with a thin layer of metal using an electric current. It is used to prevent corrosion, improve appearance, or increase wear resistance, for example chrome-plated taps or silver-plated cutlery.

## How electroplating works
The object to be plated is the cathode, connected to the negative terminal of a power supply. The anode is the plating metal, and both sit in an electrolyte containing ions of that metal. Metal ions gain electrons at the cathode and deposit as a solid layer.

## What happens at the electrodes
At the anode, metal atoms lose electrons and dissolve as positive ions, an oxidation reaction. At the cathode, metal ions gain electrons and are deposited as metal atoms, a reduction reaction. The electrolyte concentration stays roughly constant because the anode replaces what the cathode removes.

## Faraday's laws of electrolysis
The mass of metal deposited is proportional to the quantity of electric charge passed through the cell. For a given charge, the mass deposited is also proportional to the metal's molar mass divided by the number of electrons per ion.

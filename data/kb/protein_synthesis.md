# Protein Synthesis

## Overview of protein synthesis
Protein synthesis is how cells build proteins from instructions stored in DNA. It has two main stages, transcription in the nucleus and translation at the ribosome, and follows the flow DNA to RNA to protein.

## Transcription
During transcription the enzyme RNA polymerase reads a gene on DNA and builds a matching messenger RNA (mRNA) strand. In eukaryotes the mRNA is then processed and leaves the nucleus through nuclear pores.

## Translation
In translation a ribosome reads the mRNA three bases at a time, in units called codons. Transfer RNA (tRNA) molecules bring the amino acid that matches each codon, and the ribosome joins the amino acids into a growing chain.

## The genetic code
Each codon of three bases specifies one amino acid, or a start or stop signal. The code is nearly universal across living things, and several codons can code for the same amino acid, which is why it is called degenerate.

## Folding and function
After translation the amino acid chain folds into a specific three-dimensional shape. That shape determines the protein's function, such as an enzyme speeding up a reaction or a structural protein giving strength to tissue.
